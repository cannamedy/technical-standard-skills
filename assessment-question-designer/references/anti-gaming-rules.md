# Anti-Gaming and MCQ Construction Rules

## Option construction

For a standard MCQ4:

- use exactly four options unless the user requests another format;
- provide one best answer;
- keep grammar, punctuation, semantic granularity, specificity, and style parallel;
- keep lengths comparable where practical, without forcing identical character counts;
- do not make the correct option systematically longer, more qualified, or more technical;
- avoid formatting, capitalization, terminology, or stem-word clues;
- rewrite the stem when two options are defensible.

Option length is a heuristic. A warning threshold such as max length > 1.5 × min length is a signal
for review, not a rigid natural-language law. Compare the correct option with the median distractor
length as well.

## Answer position balance

Design position distribution at question-set level. For 20 four-option items, target A/B/C/D = 5/5/5/5;
otherwise counts SHOULD differ by at most one. A same-answer streak MUST NOT exceed two unless the
user explicitly requests an unbalanced or preserved sequence. Avoid repeated ABCD or DCBA cycles:
balanced ≠ predictable.

## Leakage and guessing cues

Audit for:

1. correct answer systematically longest;
2. correct answer systematically most technical or qualified;
3. correct answer grammatically unique;
4. only correct option repeating stem wording;
5. absolute words concentrated in distractors;
6. biased answer positions;
7. predictable answer-position sequence;
8. absurd distractors;
9. one option subsuming another;
10. overlapping options;
11. two defensibly correct options;
12. negative stems that create reading traps;
13. answers revealed by another item;
14. answer inferred from option structure;
15. nuanced conjunction or exception clues that only one option mirrors;
16. distractors much shorter than the correct option;
17. punctuation or capitalization clues;
18. numerical ordering that creates a clue;
19. distractors depending on facts absent from a source-grounded source;
20. multiple constructs hidden in one item;
21. semantic granularity or specificity mismatch;
22. option-order clues, such as chronology or magnitude ordering;
23. duplicated stem wording that makes the key echo the prompt;
24. tail quality degradation that turns later items into recall.

Warn on absolute wording such as always, never, only, all, none, and all/none-of-the-above. These
words are not automatically invalid; judge whether they leak or distort the intended construct.

## Batch quality

Do not let item quality decline with item index. Create the full blueprint, draft in batches, audit
the complete set, and inspect the final 25% for difficulty, source grounding, and distinct distractor
models. If the tail becomes definition recall, rewrite it rather than lowering the target.
